🏠 Project Overview

Substation Equipment Upload to Procore

PRJ-2024-001
June 1, 2025
August 29, 2025
Problem Statement
Currently, substation equipment data is not systematically uploaded into Procore, leading to gaps in project documentation, difficulty tracking asset status, and increased risk of data loss or miscommunication between field teams and project managers.
Business Case
Procore serves as the single source of truth for project documentation. Establishing a repeatable, efficient process for uploading substation equipment data will reduce rework, improve compliance, and enable real-time visibility for stakeholders — directly supporting project delivery timelines and quality standards.
✅ In Scope
• Upload process for all substation equipment types • Template development for Procore data entry • Training materials for field staff • QA/QC checklist for data accuracy • Process documentation and SOPs
🚫 Out of Scope
• Procore software customization or development • Equipment procurement or sourcing • Non-substation equipment categories • IT infrastructure changes • Third-party system integrations
Project Goals
1. Establish a standardized, repeatable upload process within 30 days 2. Achieve 100% substation equipment coverage in Procore by project end 3. Reduce data entry errors by ≥50% vs. current ad-hoc approach 4. Deliver training to all relevant team members before go-live 5. Create documentation that enables process handoff to the operations team

📋 A3 Problem Solving

1
Background
The organization uses Procore as its primary project management platform. Substation equipment records are currently managed through disconnected spreadsheets and informal processes, creating documentation gaps and accountability issues.
2
Current Condition
Equipment data is manually tracked in Excel. No standardized fields or templates exist. Upload frequency is ad-hoc, leading to outdated records. Field teams and PMs reference different versions of data, causing miscommunication and rework.
3
Target Condition
A defined, repeatable process exists for uploading substation equipment into Procore. All equipment records are current, accurate, and accessible in real-time. Team members follow a standard SOP with <5% error rate.
4
Root Cause Analysis
Primary root cause: No documented upload process or template exists. Contributing causes: - Lack of ownership/accountability for data entry - Training gap on Procore equipment module - No defined data fields or standards - Competing priorities reduce time allocated to data entry
5
Countermeasures
1. Develop standardized upload template with required fields 2. Create SOP and training materials for Procore equipment module 3. Assign data entry ownership to specific roles 4. Implement QA checklist and review cycle 5. Establish regular cadence for data updates
6
Action Plan
Week 1-2: Define scope, map current process, conduct interviews Week 3-4: Develop template and SOP Week 5-6: Pilot with one substation project Week 7-8: Refine, train team, document lessons learned Week 9-10: Full rollout and handoff
7
Follow Up / Lessons Learned
To be completed upon project closure. Review KPIs against targets, document what worked and what didn't, and identify opportunities for continuous improvement in the next project cycle.

Measure › Measurement System Analysis

🔬 Measurement System Analysis (MSA)

What Are We Measuring?
1. Upload completion rate (% of equipment in Procore vs. site register) 2. Data accuracy rate (% of records with all required fields correctly filled) 3. Upload cycle time (minutes from equipment receipt to Procore confirmation)
Measurement Method
1. Procore vs. physical asset register comparison — weekly 2. Random audit of 10% of records — spot check required fields 3. Timestamp tracking from equipment receipt form to Procore entry
GRR Assessment (Gauge Repeatability & Reproducibility)
Repeatability: Same person measuring twice — confirm same result Reproducibility: Two different people measuring same item Accuracy: Compare against known baseline (100% manually verified records) Stability: Check over 4-week period to detect drift
